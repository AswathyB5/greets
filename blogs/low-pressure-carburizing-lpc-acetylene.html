Low-Pressure Carburizing (LPC) with Acetylene: Eliminating IGO in Gears

Greets Sep 30, 2026 9 min read

In traditional atmospheric gas carburizing using endothermic gas, internal oxidation (intergranular oxidation or IGO) along gear flanks and tooth roots severely reduces tooth bending fatigue strength. For transmission gears in electric vehicles (EVs) and heavy automotive powertrains, eliminating IGO is vital to handle elevated torque densities.

Key Metallurgy Advantage In BMI ALLCARB® vacuum low-pressure carburizing, pure acetylene (C2H2) cracking occurs in an oxygen-free environment, completely eliminating IGO and boosting fatigue life by 25% to 40%.

1. The Chemistry of Acetylene Injection

Unlike propane (C3H8), which tends to crack in the gas phase and create soot, acetylene disassociates catalytically directly on the hot steel surface:

C2H2 → 2 C(austenite) + H2(gas)

This allows rapid carbon transfer and uniform case depth even in narrow gear root fillets and small blind oil passages.

2. Boost-Diffuse Pulsing Cycles

By alternating short acetylene boost injection pulses with vacuum diffusion periods, the surface carbon concentration is precisely managed to avoid cementite network precipitation. Effective case depths from 0.4 mm up to 3.0 mm can be tailored with sub-micron repeatability.
